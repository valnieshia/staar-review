📋 Program Overview & TEKS Prioritization

Student Population

This 5-day STAAR Math review is designed for a special education small group pull-out averaging 50 minutes per session. Students range from Intellectual Disability (ID) to Other Health Impairment (OHI) for ADHD. All instruction is culturally responsive and contextualized for students in Dallas, Texas.

Instructional Framework

🧠
Evidence-Based Approaches Used: Explicit Instruction (I Do → We Do → You Do), Backward-Faded Worked Examples (Cognitive Load Theory), Procedural Checklists (Proceduralizing Strategy), Schema-Based Instruction, Universal Design for Learning (UDL), Culturally Responsive Teaching (CRT), and STAAR Desmos Calculator Integration (the same calculator students use on test day).

♿ Universal Accommodations & Supports

Accommodations by Disability Category

🧩 For Students with ID (Intellectual Disability)

  • Simplified language — Rewrite word problems at lower reading level
  • Reduced number of answer choices — Highlight 2 of 4 options when possible during practice
  • Concrete manipulatives — Algebra tiles, base-ten blocks, number lines
  • Visual step-by-step checklists with pictures/icons at each step
  • Extended time (1.5x–2x on all tasks)
  • Oral administration — Read problems aloud; allow oral responses
  • Calculator access for all computation
  • Reduced problem sets (e.g., 5 problems instead of 10)
  • Enlarged font (16pt+) with increased spacing
  • Color-coded reference sheets at desk
  • Repeated practice with same problem type before moving on

⚡ For Students with OHI/ADHD

  • Chunked tasks — Break worksheets into 3-4 problem sections
  • Movement breaks — 2-3 min every 12-15 min
  • Fidget tools allowed during instruction
  • Visual timer displayed for each task segment
  • Preferential seating — Near teacher, away from distractions
  • Written + verbal directions simultaneously
  • Graphic organizers pre-printed (no copying from board)
  • Frequent check-ins — Every 3-4 problems
  • Choice in order of problems to solve
  • Token/point system for on-task behavior
  • Noise-reducing headphones during independent work
  • Highlight key words in problems before solving

Universal Supports for ALL Students in This Group

🌐 Applied Every Day

  • ✅ STAAR Reference Chart available at all times (TEA Reference Materials)
  • ✅ STAAR Desmos Calculator used in every session — the SAME calculator students will have on test day (STAAR Desmos Texas Testing Version). Students practice entering formulas using the tilde (~) regression method so they can let Desmos solve for unknowns.
  • ✅ Procedural checklist for every problem type — laminated on desk
  • ✅ Faded worked examples before independent practice
  • ✅ Sentence stems for math talk ("I noticed that…", "My first step is…")
  • ✅ Anchor charts posted on wall for each day's concept
  • ✅ Error analysis journals — students identify and correct mistakes
  • ✅ Positive reinforcement — Praise effort, not just accuracy
  • ✅ Culturally relevant contexts — Dallas landmarks, local food, community data

📘 Day 1 — Proportional Relationships & Slope

TEKS 8.4(B) & 8.4(C) | Readiness Standards | "How fast is Dallas growing?"

📎 Day 1 Materials: 🖥️ Slide Deck 📄 Worksheet

🎯 Learning Objectives

  • Students will graph a proportional relationship and identify the unit rate as slope.
  • Students will determine slope and y-intercept from a table or graph.

🌍 Culturally Responsive Context

🏙️
Dallas Connection: Students explore proportional relationships using real scenarios: cost of DART bus passes per ride, price per taco at local Dallas taquerías, miles walked on the Katy Trail per hour, and growth of the Dallas Mavericks' win record over weeks. Data reflects students' everyday experiences in their Dallas community.

⏱ 50-Minute Schedule

0:00 – 0:05
Warm-Up / Hook (5 min) — "How much does a DART day pass cost? If you ride 3 times, how much per ride?" Quick-write or partner chat. Display DART pricing visual. ADHD: Movement — students stand to share answers
0:05 – 0:20
I Do — Explicit Instruction (15 min)
  • Model: "Tacos cost $2 each at a local taquería. Let's make a table & graph."
  • Build table: 1 taco = $2, 2 = $4, 3 = $6
  • Connect: "Slope IS the unit rate — how much per one!"
  • Use anchor chart: Slope = Rise ÷ Run = Δy ÷ Δx
  • DESMOS The Desmos Table + Regression Method — "Let Desmos find the equation!"

🖩 Desmos Steps — Finding Slope from a Table

1: Pick any TWO points from the table
2: In Desmos, press the + (plus sign) button
3: Click on "table" — a table appears!
4: Type your two points into the table (x₁, y₁) and (x₂, y₂)
5: Click the "Add regression" symbol — Desmos gives you the equation!
💡 Desmos shows: y = 2x → The number in front of x IS the slope! "m = 2 means $2 per taco."
⚠️ Common student confusion: Some students struggle telling apart m (slope) and b (y-intercept) in y = mx + b. Remind them: "m is the MULTIPLIER — the number TOUCHING the x. b is the BONUS — the number all by itself."
ID: Use physical graph paper and colored dots alongside Desmos
0:20 – 0:30
We Do — Guided Practice with Faded Examples (10 min)
  • Problem: "Jaylen walks on the Katy Trail. He walks 3 miles per hour."
  • Students fill in partially completed table and graph using the faded example below
  • Use procedural checklist to verify each step
ADHD: 2-min movement break after this segment
0:30 – 0:42
You Do — Independent Practice (12 min)
  • 3-4 problems (reduced set for ID) using Dallas contexts
  • Checklist at desk; calculator available; teacher circulates
  • Problems: Mavericks ticket prices per game, cost of elotes per cup
ID: Reduced to 2-3 problems; enlarged font; read aloud
0:42 – 0:50
Exit Ticket & Closure (8 min)
  • 2 STAAR-format questions (see below)
  • Review answers together; celebrate effort
  • Preview: "Tomorrow we learn to write the EQUATION for these relationships!"

📗 Day 2 — Writing & Solving Linear Equations

TEKS 8.5(I) & 8.8(C) | Readiness Standards | "Building equations from real life"

📎 Day 2 Materials: 🖥️ Slide Deck 📄 Worksheet

🎯 Learning Objectives

  • Students will write a linear equation in y = mx + b form from a table, graph, or scenario.
  • Students will solve one-variable equations with variables on both sides.

🌍 Culturally Responsive Context

🎶
Dallas Connection: Students compare cell phone plans (T-Mobile vs. AT&T monthly costs with fees), calculate earnings from a weekend job at a Dallas flea market (La Pulga), and determine how many rides at the State Fair of Texas they can afford with different ticket packages. Real-world scenarios from students' lives make abstract equations tangible.

⏱ 50-Minute Schedule

0:00 – 0:05
Warm-Up (5 min) — Review: "What is slope? What is y-intercept?" Use hand signals (thumbs up = I remember, sideways = kinda, down = need help). Quick slope review with 2 table problems from Day 1.
0:05 – 0:20
I Do — Explicit Instruction (15 min)
  • Part A: Writing y = mx + b
  • Scenario: "You work at La Pulga and earn $10/hour plus $20 for setting up the booth."
  • m (slope) = $10/hour, b (y-intercept) = $20 start → y = 10x + 20
  • From a table: find slope (Day 1 skill), find b where x = 0
  • Part B: Solving equations with variables on both sides
  • Example: "Two phone plans: Plan A costs 15x + 30, Plan B costs 10x + 55. When are they equal?"
  • 15x + 30 = 10x + 55 → Model step-by-step with algebra tiles
  • DESMOS The Desmos Tilde Method for Solving Equations:

🖩 Desmos Steps — Solving Equations with Variables on Both Sides

1: 15a + 30 ~ 10a + 55   ← Type the WHOLE equation using ~ (tilde, NOT equals!)
2: Desmos shows: a = 5   ← That's the answer!
⚠️ CRITICAL RULE — NO "x"! Students CANNOT use the letter "x" when solving equations this way. Desmos treats x as a graphing variable. Change x to ANY other letter (a, b, n, t, etc.) and Desmos will solve it! We use "a" in class.
💡 "Both plans cost the same at 5 months! We just typed ONE line and Desmos solved the whole thing!"
  • DESMOS Writing equations: After finding m and b using the table + regression method (Day 1 skill), type the equation in Desmos → Does the line pass through all our table points? If yes, our equation is correct!
ID: Use color-coded equation mats (variables = green, constants = blue); Desmos as visual check
0:20 – 0:22
Movement Break (2 min) — Stand, stretch, "Equation Shake" (shake body for each step you remember) ADHD: Essential reset
0:22 – 0:35
We Do — Guided Practice (13 min)
  • Faded worked examples for both writing equations AND solving
  • Students use procedural checklists at desks
  • State Fair context: "Admission is $18, and each ride costs $4. Write an equation for total cost."
  • Solve: "When does Plan A = Plan B?" with guided steps
0:35 – 0:43
You Do — Independent Practice (8 min)
  • 3 problems: 1 write equation, 1 solve equation, 1 combined
  • Checklist on desk; teacher supports as needed
ID: 2 problems; read aloud; allow oral explanation of steps
0:43 – 0:50
Exit Ticket & Closure (7 min)

📙 Day 3 — Functions & Scatterplots

TEKS 8.5(G) & 8.5(D) | Readiness Standards | "Is this a function? What's the trend?"

📎 Day 3 Materials: 🖥️ Slide Deck 📄 Worksheet

🎯 Learning Objectives

  • Students will identify functions using ordered pairs, tables, mappings, and graphs (vertical line test).
  • Students will use a trend line to make predictions from scatterplot data.

🌍 Culturally Responsive Context

🏀
Dallas Connection: Students analyze whether real data is a function: Dallas Mavericks player stats (does each jersey number map to one player?), student survey data (shoe size vs. height for classmates), and local temperature trends in Dallas by month. Scatterplot data uses real Dallas weather data and local restaurant ratings.

⏱ 50-Minute Schedule

0:00 – 0:05
Warm-Up (5 min) — "Function or Not?" card sort activity. Students sort 4 tables into "Function" and "Not a Function" piles at their desks. ADHD: Hands-on sorting = kinesthetic engagement
0:05 – 0:18
I Do — Explicit Instruction: Functions (13 min)
  • "A function is like a vending machine — each button gives you EXACTLY one item."
  • Show 4 representations: ordered pairs, table, mapping, graph
  • Key rule: Each input (x) can only have ONE output (y)
  • Vertical Line Test on graph: "If a vertical line touches the graph more than once, it's NOT a function."
  • Dallas context: "Each student ID number → one student name = function. One phone number → multiple people = NOT a function."
ID: Use color coding — green border = function, red border = not
0:18 – 0:20
Movement Break (2 min) — "Stand if it's a function, sit if it's not" — teacher shows quick examples ADHD: Total Physical Response engagement
0:20 – 0:30
I Do / We Do — Scatterplots & Trend Lines (10 min)
  • Show scatterplot of Dallas monthly temperatures vs. ice cream sales
  • DESMOS Build a scatterplot in Desmos: Press the + (plus sign) → click "table". Enter x-values (temperatures) and y-values (ice cream sales). Desmos plots the points automatically!
  • DESMOS Add a trend line: Click the "Add regression" symbol → Desmos draws the best-fit line AND gives you the equation! Remember: m = MULTIPLIER (slope), b = BONUS (y-intercept). Use the line to predict: "If it's 95°F in July, trace up to the line — how many ice cream cones?"
  • Key vocab: positive association, negative association, no association
  • Guided practice with faded example
0:30 – 0:42
You Do — Independent Practice (12 min)
  • 3-4 mixed problems: identify functions (2) + scatterplot prediction (1-2)
  • Checklist at desk; teacher circulates for check-ins every 3-4 problems
ID: 2 problems; visual aids with arrows on mapping diagrams
0:42 – 0:50
Exit Ticket & Closure (8 min)

📕 Day 4 — Pythagorean Theorem & Transformations

TEKS 8.7(C) & 8.10(A) | Readiness Standards | "Measuring Dallas & moving shapes"

📎 Day 4 Materials: 🖥️ Slide Deck 📄 Worksheet

🎯 Learning Objectives

  • Students will use the Pythagorean Theorem (a² + b² = c²) to find missing side lengths.
  • Students will identify and describe transformations (translations, reflections, rotations).

🌍 Culturally Responsive Context

🏗️
Dallas Connection: Pythagorean Theorem applied to real Dallas scenarios: finding the diagonal distance across Klyde Warren Park, calculating the height of Reunion Tower using its shadow, measuring screen diagonals at AT&T Stadium. Transformations use Dallas map grids — translate the Cowboys star logo, reflect shapes across the Trinity River, rotate patterns from local murals in Deep Ellum.

⏱ 50-Minute Schedule

0:00 – 0:05
Warm-Up (5 min) — Show a photo of Reunion Tower. "If the tower casts a shadow 300 feet long and a wire from the top to the end of the shadow is 500 feet... how tall is the tower?" Activate curiosity. ADHD: Visual hook with real photo
0:05 – 0:18
I Do — Pythagorean Theorem with STAAR Desmos (13 min)
  • Reference chart connection: a² + b² = c² — "It's ON your reference chart!"
  • Label: a = leg, b = leg, c = hypotenuse (LONGEST side, across from the right angle)
  • Anchor chart: "Hypotenuse Hunter" — Always identify the hypotenuse FIRST
  • Model: "A TV at AT&T Stadium is 48 inches wide and 36 inches tall. What's the diagonal?"
  • DESMOS The Desmos Method — "Let Desmos do the math!"

🖩 Desmos Steps — Pythagorean Theorem

1: a² + b² ~ c²   ← Type the formula using ~ (tilde, not equals!)
2: a = 48   ← Plug in the first leg
3: b = 36   ← Plug in the second leg
4: c =   ← Leave this BLANK — Desmos will solve for c!
💡 Teacher tip: When you leave one variable blank, Desmos uses regression (~) to solve for it! The answer appears as a slider value. For this problem, Desmos shows c = 60 inches. "See? Desmos did the squaring, adding, AND square root for us!"
💡 Finding a LEG instead? Same method! If c = 13 and a = 5, just type: Line 1: a² + b² ~ c², Line 2: a = 5, Line 3: b = (leave blank), Line 4: c = 13 → Desmos gives you b = 12!
ID: Provide pre-labeled triangle diagrams; teacher projects Desmos on screen while students follow along on their devices

🖩 Desmos Steps — Volume of a Cylinder

"A Dallas water tank is a cylinder with radius 5 ft and height 12 ft. Find the volume."

Step 1: Find the formula on reference chart → V = Bh (for cylinder)

Step 2: Ask — "What does B mean?" → B = Area of the Base → "What shape is the base of a cylinder?" → A CIRCLE! → "What's the area of a circle?" → πr²

Step 3: Type it all into Desmos:

1: V ~ B · h   ← The volume formula (use ~ tilde!)
2: V =   ← Leave blank — this is what we're SOLVING for!
3: B ~ π · r²   ← B = Area of the Base = area of a circle!
4: h = 12   ← Plug in the height
5: r = 5   ← Plug in the radius
💡 Desmos calculates: B = π(5²) = 78.54, then V = 78.54 × 12 = V ≈ 942.48 ft³. "We just told Desmos WHAT the formulas are and WHAT the values are, and it did ALL the math!"

🖩 Desmos Steps — Volume of a Cone

"A raspa cone from a Dallas raspas stand has r = 3 in, h = 6 in."

"What's different about a cone? → V = ⅓Bh (it's one-third!) → Base is still a circle!"

1: V ~ (1/3) · B · h   ← Cone formula has the ⅓!
2: V =   ← Leave blank to solve
3: B ~ π · r²   ← Base is still a circle
4: h = 6
5: r = 3
💡 Desmos gives us V ≈ 56.55 in³. "The cone holds way less than a cylinder because of that ⅓!"

📝 Faded Worked Examples — Design Principles

What Are Faded Worked Examples?

Faded worked examples are an evidence-based scaffolding strategy grounded in Cognitive Load Theory. They reduce the demand on working memory by gradually removing solution steps, requiring students to complete increasingly more of the problem independently.

🧠
How Backward Fading Works:
  • Problem 1: ALL steps shown (fully worked) — student studies it
  • Problem 2: Last 1-2 steps removed — student completes them
  • Problem 3: Last 3-4 steps removed — student does most of the work
  • Problem 4: Only the problem is given — full independence
This backward fading has been shown to be more effective than forward fading for students with learning disabilities.

Key for Color-Coded Steps

GREEN = Given — Step is fully completed for the student
YELLOW = Partial — Part is given, student fills in the rest
RED = Blank — Student completes the entire step independently
💡
Teacher Tips:
  • For students with ID: Stay on Problem 1 and 2 longer; may need 3-4 fully worked examples before fading
  • For students with ADHD: The step-by-step format helps maintain focus; use a pointer or finger to track each step
  • Pair with procedural checklists so students can self-monitor which step they're on
  • Use think-alouds during I Do: verbalize your reasoning at each step

🎯 STAAR Test-Taking Strategies

🧩 Strategies for Students with ID

  • 1. "Screen to Scratch" — Since the test is online, you can't highlight or underline on screen. Instead, write down your keywords on scratch paper. Draw the shapes, tables, or diagrams you see. Your scratch paper IS your workspace!
  • 2. "Find It on the Chart" — Before solving, check the reference chart. If a formula is needed, it's there!
  • 3. "Read Twice, Solve Once" — Read the problem. Read it again. THEN solve.
  • 4. "Plug In the Answers" — Try each answer choice in the problem to see which one works.
  • 5. "Fold into 8 Boxes" — Fold your scratch paper into 8 boxes. Each problem gets its own box to stay organized.
  • 6. "Skip and Come Back" — If stuck, put a star ★ and move to the next one. Come back later.
  • 7. "No Blanks" — Always choose an answer, even if guessing. Eliminate one obviously wrong answer first.

⚡ Strategies for Students with ADHD

  • 1. "Screen to Scratch" — Write down keywords from the screen onto your scratch paper. When possible, draw the shapes, tables, or other important info you see. Your scratch paper is your workspace!
  • 2. "5-5-5 Rule" — Work for 5 problems, take 5 deep breaths, then do 5 more.
  • 3. "Fold Your Paper into 8 Boxes" — Fold scratch paper into 8 boxes. Do the work for each problem in its own box — keeps things organized!
  • 4. "Touch Each Answer" — Point to each answer choice and ask "Could this be right?" before selecting.
  • 5. "Body Check" — Every 10 questions, check your body: feet on floor, sit up straight, take 3 breaths.
  • 6. "Flag and Move" — Use the online flag tool to mark questions to review; don't get stuck.
